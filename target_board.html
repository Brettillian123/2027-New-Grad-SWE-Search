01

Three coverage bugs found and fixed — the board was missing most of the market

Brett noticed almost every application he had submitted was on Greenhouse. That was right, and the cause was worse than a Greenhouse bias. The scanner pre-filtered every platform with a rule that required an explicit new grad signal in the title or body. Measured against a corpus collected without it, that filter discards 87% of eligible postings — 2,440 of 2,789, including 343 plain Software Engineer reqs, plus Full Stack, Data, SRE, Platform, ML and Forward Deployed Engineer. Every board scanned before today was missing them.

02

Workday was 2.4% of the scan and is the largest ATS in the market

In a 19,178-row sample of live postings, Workday appears 8,621 times against Greenhouse's 1,533 — yet the original scan covered 336 Greenhouse companies and only 22 on Workday. The cause was token discovery: Greenhouse needs one guessable slug, Workday needs a tenant, a numbered pod and an arbitrary site path, and its job list is a POST. 1,314 valid Workday board addresses were already sitting in harvested data and had never been read back out. Re-scanning them returned 2,789 eligible postings across 466 companies, against 59 across 22 before.

03

Every Illinois location counted as Chicago

The geography rule matched illinois and a bare IL, so Peoria, Springfield, Rockford, Champaign and Shiloh — a St. Louis suburb 280 miles away — all flagged as Chicago, and so did Normal. It is now a 51-term Chicago-metro list verified against 14 cases. The Chicago column on the previous board was inflated by the width of the whole state.

04

Salary bands were being thrown away before they could be read

Descriptions were truncated to 1,500 characters at collection time, and pay bands sit at the end of a posting. Only Ashby survived, because it returns structured compensation as a separate field. Extracting pay from the full text before truncating recovered a band on 53 of 120 re-scanned postings that had shown none.

05

What the gates cost, and where the board now stands

Across 7,524 eligible postings from 3,680 boards, the two-week freshness rule removed 6,058 — by far the largest single cut. Requiring an actual entry-level req — no title a level above it, no stated floor of two years or more — removed 0, and the remote-or-Chicago rule 1,178 more. 52 companies survive everything. A dash in the pay column means the employer published no band, which is common on Workday and is not the same as paying badly; those rows are kept.

Read this before you apply

  • Several top quant firms screen on GPA, commonly at 3.5. Akuna, Belvedere, Chicago Trading Company, Old Mission, TransMarket, Wolverine, SpiderRock and Optiver are the more accessible entries; Citadel, Jane Street, HRT, Two Sigma and Jump are the tightest.
  • Trading firms hire on a rolling basis and close requisitions once a class fills. In this segment, applying in the first two weeks matters more than a perfectly tailored resume.
  • Citadel and Citadel Securities auto-refresh the datePosted field on their own pages daily, so their dates are a freshness stamp rather than a real open date. Both requisitions are genuinely live.
  • A dash in the pay column means the employer published no band, not that it pays poorly. Any role with a published band below $95,000 was filtered out; a handful of ranges shown start lower but top out well above the floor.
  • Nothing here records work authorization or sponsorship, and several quant and defense-adjacent firms have hard restrictions. Confirm before investing time.
  • Dates are the employer's own first_published / publishedAt / startDate field read from its applicant-tracking system, or a quoted first-party page. Requisitions still close without notice — confirm on the linked page before applying.
